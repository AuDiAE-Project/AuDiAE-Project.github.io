AuDiAE:Learning Audio Latents by Coupling Diffusion Priors and Decoders

AuDiAE couples a learned diffusion rate model with a flow-matching waveform decoder through a shared encoder representation. The marginal rate bounds total latent information, while its text-conditional counterpart makes caption-predictable information less costly to encode.

01 Method

An audio latent serves two consumers: the decoder that reconstructs the waveform, and the downstream generator that learns to produce latents from text. The standard design serves both with a Gaussian KL penalty and a deterministic decoder; AuDiAE replaces them with a learned diffusion rate model and a waveform diffusion decoder around a shared encoder output.

MotivationThree limitations of the standard design

  1. ①

    Standard KL also charges for mismatch

    The KL to 𝒩(0, I) adds two costs: the information the latent carries about the audio, and the mismatch between the latent distribution and 𝒩(0, I). Only the first serves reconstruction, yet under a tight budget the second consumes rate that could carry detail (illustrated and measured below).

    Addressed by A · learned diffusion rate
  2. ②

    Latent detail the caption cannot predict

    A text-to-audio generator learns latents given a caption, so its target is the latent information the caption leaves unexplained. Latents with the same total information can pose different learning problems.

    Addressed by B · text-conditional rate
  3. ③

    One latent, many waveforms

    At 2048–4096× compression a rate-limited latent is compatible with many waveforms. A deterministic decoder must return one of them, collapsing this one-to-many inverse into a point estimate.

    Addressed by C · waveform diffusion decoder

ApproachHow AuDiAE addresses them

  1. A · addresses ①

    Learned marginal diffusion rate

    Replace the fixed Gaussian with a learned reference. A fixed-precision channel turns the encoder output U into the decoder condition Zb, and a diffusion prior with the null condition models this endpoint. Its variational bound still upper-bounds the transmitted information, but the reference now follows the aggregate latent distribution, so the mismatch Mu can shrink instead of competing with detail.

    Controlled evidence · Table 3(a): replacing the fixed Gaussian rate with the learned rate raises SI-SDR by 1.07 dB, lowers rFAD by 17% and lowers generation FAD from 0.142 to 0.093.

  2. B · addresses ②

    Text-conditional mixed rate

    Give the same prior the frozen caption feature C and mix the conditional rate with the marginal one. The mixture subtracts ρI(Zb;C), so information the caption can predict becomes cheaper to encode, while the marginal part still bounds total information. No latent coordinate is aligned to a text embedding, and the encoder sees only audio.

    Controlled evidence · Table 3(b): paired captions lower generation FAD to 0.075, whereas shuffled captions give 0.095, no better than the unconditional rate (0.093).

  3. C · addresses ③

    Waveform diffusion decoder

    Treat decoding as conditional waveform generation. A clean-target flow-matching decoder samples a waveform consistent with Zb rather than returning one point estimate, and its denoising risk values encoded detail by how much it reduces synthesis uncertainty. Generative decoding cannot recover source information absent from Zb.

    Listen · reconstruction examples at 2048× (AuDiAE) and 4096× (AuDiAE-H).

Joint training

Encoder, rate model and decoder are optimized together; the multiresolution STFT term is a practical spectral prior outside the variational derivation. Stage 1 uses only the null condition (ρ = 0), and Stage 2 adds paired captions (ρ > 0). For audio generation, a separate generator is trained on the frozen encoder output U.
